Dissecting a winning team's passing game

4–0 Inter Miami beat Toronto FC · 21 September 2023 · MLS Regular Season

Every completed pass Inter Miami made that night, mapped as a network and read for who actually controlled the game — not just who scored.

01

Two players ran the network, well clear of everyone else

Ranking every player by how central they were to the passing network for the match:

Sergio Busquets and Facundo Farías topped the team by a clear margin, with Serhiy Kryvtsov, DeAndre Yedlin, and Kamal Miller close behind. That's not just "touched the ball a lot" — centrality measures how connected a player is to the rest of the team's passing structure, and these two sat above everyone else in the squad.

02

The busiest single connection was the two centre-backs

Looking at exactly which pairs of players exchanged the ball most across the 90 minutes:

Kryvtsov and Miller combined for more passes between them than any other pair on the pitch, with Yedlin-Taylor on the right and Busquets-Miller close behind. Before Inter Miami built anything forward, the ball usually ran through this pairing first.

03

The players circulating the ball weren't always the ones creating shots

Cross-referencing the network above against which passes actually led to a shot:

Busquets shows up in both lists — the connective core and a direct shot creator. But Jordi Alba, David Ruiz, and Robert Taylor also set up shots despite not being the highest-volume passers in the network above — including a pass that found Messi in the box. Controlling possession and creating danger weren't always the same job on this team.

04

Where possession actually landed

Mapping every pass reception across the pitch for the full match:

Reception density clusters through the center and the right side, matching the Kryvtsov-Miller and Yedlin-Taylor combinations above — the heaviest traffic wasn't evenly spread, it ran through the same handful of lanes the pairing data already pointed to.

Five names came up across every angle of this analysis — Busquets, Farías, Kryvtsov, Yedlin, and Miller — the players an opponent would need to disrupt to break Inter Miami's passing structure, not just the most visible names on the scoresheet.
