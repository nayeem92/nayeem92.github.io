Season analysis · Bundesliga 2023/24

What actually held Bayer Leverkusen's unbeaten season together

Leverkusen went 34 matches without a loss — the first team in Bundesliga history to do it. This breaks down what the tracking data says actually sustained that, testing the popular explanations rather than repeating them.

01

The two wing-backs were never running the same job

A back four with two attacking full-backs looks symmetrical on a formation sheet. It wasn't, in practice.

Jeremie Frimpong was the overlapping box-crasher — 87 entries into the penalty box across the season, more than five times Alejandro Grimaldo's 15. Grimaldo's value showed up elsewhere: nearly double the ball carries (1,662 vs. 1,019) and a near-equal number of crosses (106 vs. 95). Same nominal role on the team sheet, two specifically different jobs.

02

Central midfield wasn't an overload — it was a division of labor

The common explanation for Leverkusen's central control is "extra bodies overwhelming the opposition." Checked directly against tracking data, that framing doesn't hold — they don't consistently outnumber opponents centrally during buildup.

What actually explains it: a custom measure of opponents eliminated per pass — real tracked defenders standing between the start and end of a pass, taken out of the game by that single pass. Granit Xhaka led the entire team by a wide margin (940 total, next closest 487) — always available, always progressing play. Florian Wirtz attempted barely a third of that volume, but eliminated opponents at nearly double the rate per pass (0.92 vs. 0.74). Two different, statistically distinct ways of doing the same job — not one player simply doing it better than the other.

03

The defense didn't have a "big-game mode" — and didn't need one

The season's six draws came against the toughest opposition faced all year (Bayern Munich, Dortmund twice, Stuttgart twice, Mönchengladbach). Comparing those six games against the six biggest blowout wins tests whether the team changed how it defended under pressure.

It didn't. Pressing intensity, defensive line height, and defensive compactness were statistically indistinguishable between the hardest games and the routine wins — no tactical shift for the occasion. What actually changed: the draws faced nearly double the shots, genuinely harder underlying games. What closed that gap was a season-long shot-stopping margin of +3.8 goals prevented against expectation that didn't spike for big games — it was simply present every week, and happened to be almost exactly the width of the gap between drawing and losing those level games.

04

Pressing resistance only shows up possession by possession

Across a whole season, how hard an opponent pressed had essentially no relationship with how patiently Leverkusen built out (statistically close to zero at the match level). The real effect was hiding inside that average.

Counting the actual number of opponents committed inside Leverkusen's own half at the exact moment a buildup began tells a much clearer story: a clean, steady drop from an 88% escape rate down to 61% as commitment rises. And that risk wasn't shared evenly — Grimaldo and Frimpong, the two most advanced outlets in the buildup shape, had the lowest escape rate of anyone on the team, while the centre-backs and goalkeeper (usually receiving the ball with time) sat above 80%.

05

The "double 10s" shared a shape, not a job

Florian Wirtz and Jonas Hofmann are usually described as a "double 10" pairing behind the striker. The shape claim actually holds — both average almost exactly the same height on the pitch.

But the pairing wasn't interchangeable. Wirtz's strongest combination was with Grimaldo, the wing-back on his own side — 549 combined passes, 2.9x his link with Frimpong. Hofmann's strongest combination was with Frimpong on his own side too, but far less exaggerated (1.5x rather than 2.9x). Each "10" was anchored to a specific flank, and they did the job differently: Wirtz carried the ball forward himself more than twice as often as Hofmann (123 vs. 54 final-third entries by carry), while Hofmann's value showed up in combination play rather than running at defenders.

06

One player held the passing network together

Zooming out from any single pairing to the whole team's structure: a season-long passing network built only from the most-used starting XI, not every squad player who ever touched the ball.

Even inside a network where all 55 possible pairs among the regular XI exchanged at least 14 passes across the season, the two same-side links (Wirtz-Grimaldo, Hofmann-Frimpong) stand out as visibly the thickest connections on the pitch. Ranking players by network centrality settles who the system actually depends on: Xhaka, by a wide margin, on both raw pass volume and betweenness centrality (0.47 — more than double any teammate). Wirtz is the one exception to "volume predicts importance": only fifth in raw pass involvement, but second in betweenness — a disproportionate share of the team's circulation runs through him specifically, despite him not touching the ball that often.

What actually held under load

Leverkusen's unbeaten season wasn't a system that adapted itself to the occasion. Pressing intensity, defensive shape, and buildup structure barely moved between the hardest fixtures and the most routine wins. What held instead was a small set of specific, measurable specializations: a wing-back pairing built around two different jobs rather than one mirrored role; a central midfield that splits volume and efficiency across two different players rather than overloading numerically; two forward players who share a nominal position but are wired to opposite flanks and contribute differently; a passing network with one clear hub and bridge; and a modest, season-long shot-stopping margin that only had to matter in the six games that finished level.

Data: StatsBomb open data, including 360° freeze-frame tracking, for all 34 of Leverkusen's 2023/24 Bundesliga matches.
